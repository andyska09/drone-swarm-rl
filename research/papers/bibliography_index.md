# Bibliography Index

Search index for `research/papers/`. Every PDF has a `pdftotext -layout` transcript
(`.md`) next to it — grep the `.md`, open the `.pdf` only for figures.

Regenerate a transcript with:
`pdftotext -layout <file>.pdf <file>.md`

| paper | files | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| Huang et al. 2024, *Collision Avoidance and Navigation for a Quadrotor Swarm Using End-to-end DRL* ([arXiv:2309.13285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13285)) | `2309.13285v2.pdf`, `.md` | The paper behind `quad-swarm-rl`. Rotor-thrust actions, attention over K nearest neighbours, SDF obstacles, collision-episode replay. Source of the cost-style reward we copy. |
| Gavin, Lacroix & Bronz 2026, *Agile Interception of a Flying Target using Competitive Reinforcement Learning* ([arXiv:2603.16279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16279)) | `2603.16279v1.pdf`, `.md` | The 1v1 predecessor of the Nv1 paper below. Pursuer and evader both learn with PPO in a co-evolution loop, CTBR actions on a high-fidelity quadrotor model written in JAX — the same stack we are building. Adds real indoor flights, which the Nv1 paper does not have. |
| Chen, Yu et al. 2024, *What Matters in Learning a Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real RL Policy for Quadrotor Control?* ([arXiv:2412.11764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11764)) | `2412.11764v2.pdf`, `.md` | SimpleFlight: a PPO recipe for single-drone trajectory tracking, ablated factor by factor and flown on a Crazyflie. Rotation matrix + velocity in the actor, time vector in the critic only, `‖u_t − u_{t−1}‖₂` smoothness reward at λ = 0.4, SysID instead of blanket domain randomization, large batches. CTBR actions at 100 Hz — our action space. Source of the action-difference penalty in `research/notes/literature/reward_terms.md`. |
| Gavin & Bronz 2026, *Intercepting an Agile Target with Net-Carrying Drones using Competitive MARL* ([arXiv:2607.05939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05939)) | `2607.05939v1.pdf`, `.md` | Nv1 interception, both sides learned. MAPPO with CTDE, CTBR actions at 100 Hz over a 1 kHz attitude controller, Prioritized Fictitious Self-Play against a frozen opponent pool. Closest prior art to our Nv1 rung — same obs split (self / others / arena), and it terminates on pursuer-pursuer collision but keeps a soft penalty for pursuer-evader contact. |
| Pliska, Vrba, Báča & Saska 2024, *Towards Safe Mid-Air Drone Interception: Strategies for Tracking & Capture* ([arXiv:2405.13542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13542), RA-L 9(10) 8810-8817) | `2405.13542v1.pdf`, `.md` | The non-learning interception baseline, and the source of the FRPN guidance law both Gavin papers compare against. Runs on `ctu-mrs/mrs_multirotor_simulator` — the same C++ plant we reimplement. Net is a circular plane of **2 m radius** hung below the interceptor; capture is the target's centre **passing through** that plane. Evaluated on 100 target trajectories up to 10 m/s and 8 m/s², 5 random starts within 20 m, 100 s each. Baselines: pure pursuit, LPN, GPN, MPC. |
| Sánchez Roncero, Cai, Andersson & Ögren 2026, *Learned Controllers for Agile Quadrotors in Pursuit-Evasion Games* ([arXiv:2506.02849](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02849)) | `2506.02849v3.pdf`, `.md` | 1v1 pursuit-evasion, both sides learned with PPO, one side frozen while the other trains. Shows that training against only the latest opponent (AMSDRL — what our `swap_min` loop does) forgets and cycles, and replaces it with a growing population of frozen opponents picked by a Hedge sampler (AMSPBH, a PSRO variant). CTBR actions at 50 Hz over a 500 Hz rate PID, 24-dim obs (R, body v, ω, world p, body-frame Δp and Δv to the opponent). Capture is terminal, out-of-bounds and timeout are truncations, and the capture bonus shrinks above 2 m/s closing speed. Isaac Lab, 6144 envs, 13 generations × 50M frames, zero-shot to Crazyflie brushless at up to 4.87 m/s. |
| Němeček 2026, *Strategies for UAV Pursuit-Evasion Under Active Avoidance* (CTU FEL bachelor thesis, supervised by Pliska) | `F3-BP-2026-Nemecek-Vaclav-bachelor_thesis.pdf`, `.md` | 1v1, classical against learned. FRPN pursuer and an APF evader with wall repulsion, both tuned by CMA-ES, against PPO (Stable-Baselines3 on Flightsim, X500 parameters). Actions are world-frame acceleration + heading rate, not CTBR. Obs: 24-dim pursuer (t, Δp, ‖Δp‖, Δv, altitude, R, v, ω), 26-dim evader (full position instead of altitude) — the time input was needed for convergence. Capture is a 0.5 m sphere, timeout 600 steps. RL pursuer keeps 95.0% against the APF evader that drops FRPN to 61.2%. Alternating training with the opponent pool from Sánchez Roncero above (p_old = 0.75) stops the forgetting that p_old = 0 shows. Success falls as the arena grows from 20×20×10 to 160×160×80 m at fixed episode length. |
| Chou, Maturana & Scherer 2017, *Improving Stochastic Policy Gradients in Continuous Control with Deep Reinforcement Learning using the Beta Distribution* (ICML 2017, [PMLR 70](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/chou17a.html)) | `chou17a.pdf`, `chou17a-supp.pdf`, `.md` | A Gaussian policy clipped to the action bounds gives a biased policy gradient near the bounds. A Beta policy on the bounded interval is bias-free. α, β = softplus + 1, tested with TRPO and ACER on OpenAI Gym and MuJoCo tasks and an off-road driving task. The supplement holds the Fisher information of both. The source of our Beta policy. |
| Krátký, Pěnička, Gupta, Procházka & Saska 2026, *RVC-NMPC: Nonlinear Model Predictive Control with Reciprocal Velocity Constraints for Mutual Collision Avoidance in Agile UAV Flight* ([arXiv:2512.08574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08574), RA-L, [10.1109/LRA.2026.3737070](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2026.3737070)) | `2512.08574v3.pdf`, `.md` | Decentralized mutual avoidance, not learned. Each drone knows only the current position and velocity of the others, never their plans. One ORCA half-plane per neighbour, valid until the two drones would pass, goes as a soft velocity constraint into an NMPC on the full quadrotor model, at 100 Hz. 10 drones swap across a 10 m circle in 3.07 s at limits of 20 m/s and 40 m/s², 31 % under the best rival, with no collision. A 3 h reliability test flies 10 drones to random goals in a 20 × 20 × 1 m box, up to 25.9 m/s, with no violation of the minimum distance. Delay and noise sweeps on the neighbour's state. Real flights: 3 drones, up to 17.7 m/s, on the MRS UAV system. |
| Geles, Bauersfeld, Wulfmeier & Scaramuzza 2026, *Superhuman Safe and Agile Racing through Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning* ([arXiv:2605.22748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22748)) | `2605.22748v2.pdf`, `.md` | Quadrotor racing with a variable number of racers, trained with league self-play. A permutation-invariant attention encoder (Perceiver) reads the other racers, and its ablation shows that safe coordination needs it. Beats a champion human pilot above 22 m/s and halves the collision rate of single-agent baselines. A reference for attention in our network. |
| de Souza, Newbury, Cosgun, Castillo, Vidolov & Kulić 2021, *Decentralized Multi-Agent Pursuit Using Deep Reinforcement Learning* (RA-L 6(3), 4552) | `Decentralized_Multi-Agent_Pursuit_Using_Deep_Reinforcement_Learning.pdf`, `.md` | Up to 8 unicycle pursuers against a faster reactive evader. One shared TD3 policy runs on each pursuer, with local inputs, a sweeping-angle order of the neighbours, and a curriculum. The reward adds an individual term, the distance to the target, and a group term, a formation score. Flown on three real drones. A reference for how to share the reward between pursuers. |
